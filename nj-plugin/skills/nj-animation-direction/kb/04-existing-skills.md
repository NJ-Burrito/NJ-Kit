## 1. DS · wuwangzhang1216/DirectorSKILL (MIT) · `cinematic-director` v2.1.0

**Files read:** `LICENSE`, `SKILL.md` (whole), `references/blocking-and-staging.md` (headings + Thesis, Proxemics, Power geometry, patterns 1-5, Depth staging, Entrances, Eyeline, Notation, AI-video tables), `references/failure-modes.md` (order of ops, triage, cost ladder, three-strike, root-cause list, symptom index, F2-F5, Irreducible), `assets/qc-checklist.md`, `assets/beat-sheet-template.md`, `evals/run.md`, first 2.5 KB of `evals/evals.json`.
**Not read:** `references/cinematic-language.md`, `editing-and-assembly.md`, `continuity-bible.md`, `ai-video-tool-adapters.md`, `genre-playbooks.md`, `prompt-lexicon.md`, `lighting-and-color.md`, `production-workflow.md`, `sound-and-dialogue.md`, `product-and-macro.md`, all 20 `director_styles/*`, other `assets/*`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `.github/workflows/*`.

- **EX-001** · Blocking before framing: decide what bodies do in space, then place the camera; "a well-blocked scene reads even shot flat". · `SKILL.md` hard rule 2; `references/blocking-and-staging.md` Thesis (https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/DirectorSKILL/blob/main/references/blocking-and-staging.md) · MIT
- **EX-002** · Every action has an end state: "He turns" is not a shot; "he turns until his profile is against the window, then stops" is. Missing end state is code F5, and its fix is an explicit terminal sentence plus a duration that matches the action (head turn 1.5-2 s, sit-down 2.5 s). · `SKILL.md` rule 4; `failure-modes.md` F5 · MIT
- **EX-003** · Behaviour, not emotion: never prompt "he is afraid"; write what fear does to a body. F4 before/after: "realizes he was betrayed" becomes "stops chewing, sets the glass down without letting go, jaw tightens". · `SKILL.md` rule 5; `failure-modes.md` F4 · MIT
- **EX-004** · One dominant camera move and one primary action per clip; stacking unrelated actions is the same defect from the other side; no camera move is a legitimate choice. A "motion budget" = one subject action + one camera behaviour + one environmental motion. · `SKILL.md` rule 3 and Defaults · MIT
- **EX-005** · Response-size ceilings: a one-shot prompt request gets the prompt plus at most three lines of rationale; a named mode gets only that mode's deliverable; only "everything I need" gets the full pipeline. "Over-production is the most common failure of this skill." · `SKILL.md` Response size table · MIT
- **EX-006** · A beat is a change in the balance of pressure, not a camera change or a location change. Seven rules: state in differs from state out; every delta names its agent; camera is not a beat; location change is not a beat; one beat one turn; escalation not business earns a row; last beat is what the audience walks out holding. Worked weak-versus-strong row. · `assets/beat-sheet-template.md` (https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/DirectorSKILL/blob/main/assets/beat-sheet-template.md) · MIT
- **EX-007** · Pressure notation must be arithmetic: score 0-10 for the POV character, write signed deltas with their agent, split into `-6 him / +1 us` when audience and character diverge, and the running curve must equal state-in plus each delta. "A curve that does not sum is a mood board with digits." · `beat-sheet-template.md` Pressure notation · MIT
- **EX-008** · Beat-count heuristic: beats about seconds/5 up to ~40 s, widening to ~7 s per beat for 40-60 s, then stop dividing: past 60 s split into 20-40 s scenes. A "beat" under 2 s is a shot; over 10 s is two beats or dead air; one shot per beat at scene scale. · `beat-sheet-template.md` How many beats · MIT
- **EX-009** · Proxemics as subtext with numbers: four zones (intimate 0-0.45 m, personal 0.45-1.2, social 1.2-3.6, public 3.6+), each with its camera consequence. Changing zone is the loudest event; whoever initiates wants something, whoever refuses to restore distance holds power. Always write `2.4 m -> 0.6 m over 4 s`, never "moves closer". · `blocking-and-staging.md` Proxemics · MIT
- **EX-010** · Nine power devices (height differential with real eye-height gaps, level break, foreground dominance, who moves vs who holds, crossing into space, back to camera, boxed by architecture, who controls the exit, turning away mid-line). Same line, two blockings, opposite meaning. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Power geometry · MIT
- **EX-011** · A 14-pattern staging library (table opposition, approach, retreat, parallel walk, orbit, wedge, barrier, threshold, ladder, pin, pass-through …), each with plan-view diagram, what it reads as, camera placement, and an **AI reliability grade with a cheaper substitution** (orbit = low, so cut two static setups from start and end angle). I read patterns 1-5 in full and skimmed the rest. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Staging pattern library · MIT
- **EX-012** · Depth staging substitutes for coverage: inside one clip you cannot cut, but you can restage: foreground head lowers 20 degrees and the background figure becomes the subject. Working distances on 35 mm: FG 0.8-1.2 m, MG 2.5-3.5 m, BG 6 m+. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Depth staging · MIT
- **EX-013** · Entrances versus exits: exits are easy, entrances are hard because an entering body does not exist in the start frame; begin with a sliver already in frame (shoulder, shadow, reflection). Exit past the lens is the strongest but costs the most face. The empty-frame hold of 1.5-4 s makes the frame the subject. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Entrances and exits · MIT
- **EX-014** · Eyeline is an instruction to the audience: the look-then-reveal pair (face near frame edge, then the thing from the looker's height) builds geography in two clips; hold a false eyeline 1.5-3 s past comfortable and use it once; the reverse shot's camera height must match the looker's eye height. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Eyeline and attention · MIT
- **EX-015** · A compact blocking notation (`A @FL/FG:pR --[crosses right, 1.4m, 1.5s]--> @off-FR | eye: A>B @t=2.5`) used as the director's working layer, always translated into plain start -> move -> end prose in the shot row. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Blocking notation · MIT
- **EX-016** · Two honest tables: motions a generator does reliably (breathing, head turn <= 45 degrees with degrees and seconds stated, single translation <= 0.9 m, head lower/lift as "best single-beat emotional move") and unreliable ones with a story-preserving substitution (two-person choreography, handoffs, stairs, leave-and-re-enter, full 180 turn, falling). The same logic applies to any rigged-character system with a known weak set. · `blocking-and-staging.md` Blocking for AI video · MIT
- **EX-017** · A coded failure taxonomy (F1-F19) with a symptom-to-code index ("looks like a slideshow" = F2, "cup jumped to her other hand" = F7). Diagnose the mechanism before rewriting: "a rewrite that does not name the cause is a guess". F2 gives three motion layers (body, cloth/hair, environment, at least two per clip) and a weight shift. · `failure-modes.md` symptom index and F2; `SKILL.md` Gotchas · MIT
- **EX-018** · A cost ladder L1-L7 ordered by how much work each rung throws away (prompt edit, parameter, regenerate, rebuild keyframe, V2V pass, re-plan the shot, fix in the edit, cut the shot) and a **three-strike rule**: after three failures of the same shot design, change the shot not the prompt, choosing exactly one of shorten / push in / simplify / split / first-last frame / substitute / off-screen. Thresholds are pre-committed "so the decision is not made while you are frustrated" and the file admits they are "planning guidance, not measured success rates". · `failure-modes.md` Cost ladder, Three-strike rule · MIT
- **EX-019** · Multi-symptom clips: collect every code, then find the single upstream decision that produced them all (usually duration, shot size or an over-scoped action) before touching a symptom. Repair-note format: shot, code, generation count, cause, fix level, prevention step. · `failure-modes.md` Order of operations · MIT
- **EX-020** · An "irreducible failures"list with workaround and a phrasing for each (sustained hand work, hand operating a branded product, readable text, multi-person contact, long object permanence, mirror reflections, …"between the frames" on the cut. · `failure-modes.md` Irreducible failures · MIT
- **EX-021** · Three scored gates with named blockers: PRE (plan and keyframes, weights 20/15/25/20/20, generate at >= 80), POST per clip (identity 25, action 20, motion 20, camera 10, frame 15, look 10), SEQUENCE (>= 85). A clip can score 82 and still be rejected by a blocker (visible sixth finger). Watch each clip four times: speed, 0.25x, sound off, sound only. · `assets/qc-checklist.md` (https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/DirectorSKILL/blob/main/assets/qc-checklist.md) · MIT
- **EX-022** · Intake is a checklist the agent fills silently, never printed back; proceed on stated assumptions; ask at most one question and only when a wrong guess would make the whole deliverable useless. · `SKILL.md` Intake and defaults, Hard rule 11 · MIT
- **EX-023** · Style-lens precedence: director lens > genre playbook > project tone > skill defaults. One lens at a time; if the named director has no module, **say so, name the nearest module and the axis it differs on, mark an ad-hoc lens as unvetted**. Silent substitution "is worse than no lens". · `SKILL.md` Step 4 · MIT
- **EX-024** · "The keyframe is the control point": a video model mostly re-animates what the still already decided; a style lens applied to the shot list but not to the keyframe will not survive generation. · `SKILL.md` Hard rule 7, Step 8 · MIT
- **EX-025** · Evals as a behaviour contract: each case is a prompt plus 4-6 assertions checkable from the response text alone (a number in range, a verbatim string repeated, a forbidden thing absent, a named structure present). Generate in one session, grade in a second so the assertions cannot leak; one case per fresh session; a case passes only if every assertion passes. · `evals/run.md` (https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/DirectorSKILL/blob/main/evals/run.md); `evals/evals.json` · MIT
- **EX-026** · Four prompt shapes (S1 motion-only, S2 full description, S3 keyframe pair, S4 multi-shot timestamped). In S4, identity/wardrobe/palette live once in an `Overall:` block and are never restated in a segment, and no segment line carries an emotion word. · `SKILL.md` Step 10 · MIT
- **EX-027** · The visual thesis must be concrete and testable: "the boy gets smaller as the room gets more judgmental", not "loneliness and fate"; "if you cannot state it as a change you could photograph, it is not a thesis". Non-narrative work swaps it for a claim plus its single visible proof. · `SKILL.md` Step 2 · MIT

**What DS gets wrong or omits.** It is built for text-to-video and image-to-video generators; nothing on 2D/3D rigged or hand-drawn character acting, timing charts, spacing, or animatic timing in frames. Its numbers (zones, distances, pass thresholds, weights) have no cited source and it says so itself. Very large (over 1 MB of reference text) so the agent can load only slices; the skill needs its own routing table to cope. It keeps story rules and tool-specific rules apart well, but the tool rules go stale.

## 2. VS · smixs/visual-skills, `video/` skill (CC BY 4.0)

**Files read:** `LICENSE` (header), `NOTICE`, `video/SKILL.md`, `references/dramaturgy.md`, `universal-rules.md` (sections 1-12), `role-modes.md` (head), `fixes-and-skeletons.md` (sections 1-2), `animatic-keyframes.md` (sections 1-6), `patterns-and-genres.md` section 4, `camera-lighting-vocabulary.md` (head).
**Not read:** `seedance.md`, `seedance-25.md`, `kling.md`, `veo.md`, `race-and-speed.md`, rest of `patterns-and-genres.md`, the whole `image/` skill, `README*.md`.
**Credit string required when quoting:** "Serge Shima, https://github.com/smixs/visual-skills (CC BY 4.0)".

- **EX-028** · `Scene = hero's desire + obstacle + space geometry + controlled gaze + editing rhythm`; if any element is missing the scene collapses into decoration; name each in one sentence before writing a prompt. · `video/references/dramaturgy.md` s1 (https://github.com/smixs/visual-skills/blob/main/video/references/dramaturgy.md) · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-029** · Three-jobs rule: every shot must change emotion, advance action or increase pressure, otherwise delete it ("a beautiful establishing shot is not a job"). · `dramaturgy.md` s3 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-030** · Details Law / three-detail check: each shot owns one environmental pressure, one physical micro-action on the body and one sound anchor or visual motif; zero is filler, one is thin. Calibration: 2-4 observable cues per emotional transition; fewer leaves the model guessing, more reads as overacting. · `dramaturgy.md` s2; `universal-rules.md` s1, U3 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-031** · Murch's Rule of Six used as a cut-decision priority with weights (emotion 51, story 23, rhythm 10, eye-trace 7, 2D plane 5, 3D space 4). Practical consequence: cutting for "dynamism" alone sits at item 3. · `dramaturgy.md` s4 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-032** · Blocking as "choreography of desire": for every character name what they want now, whom they move toward and away from, whom they corner, to whom they yield space, and …"he stands near the window" versus good "his shoulder stays angled back toward her". · `dramaturgy.md` s5 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-033** · Camera must have a reason, tested by "what changed?"; a legitimate reason list (a decision, new information, escalating pressure, a look, a pulled focus, the space changed). Good: "push-in starts on 'I don't know' and stops on her jaw locking". · `dramaturgy.md` s7 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-034** · A 60-90 s beat map (hook, context, pressure, **crack**, acceleration, **impact**, aftermath) "adjust for 30 s or 15 s by compressing proportionally; never skip the Crack or the Impact". A worked 30 s example (guilt / phone / reflection) gives beats at 0-3, 3-10, 10-18, 18-22, 22-26, 26-28, 28-30 s. There is **no separate 15 s map**; only the proportional rule. · `dramaturgy.md` s10, s14 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-035** · Editing rhythm as a staircase ("long, shorter, shorter, pause, impact"), with named ladders for slow-burn drama, commercial arc, anxiety build and impact scene; "the pause before the impact is more important than the speed of the cuts; always insert at least one pause before the biggest cut". · `dramaturgy.md` s10 layer 3, s12 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-036** · One-anchor principle: for a short piece commit to exactly five anchors (one emotion, one visual motif, one anchor object, one break, one final image). · `dramaturgy.md` s13 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-037** · A 14-field shot card (ID, beat, emotion, frame, composition, camera, **movement reason**, action, **eye trace in the first 0.3 s**, duration, cut type, sound, light/colour, production note). "Empty fields reveal missing direction." · `dramaturgy.md` s11 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-038** · Choose the story arc before the beat map: causal chain, turn without conflict (kishotenketsu), one peak moment, conflict beat sheet, hook and open end, each with signals in the brief and "does not fit" cases. "Running two arcs at once produces a film that changes its mind in the middle." · `video/references/patterns-and-genres.md` s4 (https://github.com/smixs/visual-skills/blob/main/video/references/patterns-and-genres.md) · CC BY 4.0 (NOTICE: adapted from agentara/skills, MIT)
- **EX-039** · An animatic keyframe card for a *still* panel that must carry one beat, one function tag, one emotion and one implied motion, with three still-specific fields: **implied camera** (the move this still is the first frame of), **emotion-via-object**, **motion-as-still**. · `video/references/animatic-keyframes.md` s1-s2 (https://github.com/smixs/visual-skills/blob/main/video/references/animatic-keyframes.md) · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-040** · Density ladder: loudness in the audio equals panel count in the board. A silent beat is one held panel, a bang is a 3-9 panel burst at 8 then 4 frames, and a burst is never followed by another burst without one held quiet panel. A 30 s spot lands at about 18-26 panels as a descending duration staircase broken by one pre-launch pause and one post-finish hold. · `animatic-keyframes.md` s3 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-041** · Six frozen cues that make a still read as motion (directional smear, frozen partial blur, streak frame, posture/trajectory vector, sharp-subject/blurred-field, deliberate freeze-frame stillness) plus a speed-budget rule: a still sells velocity only when at least three of five cues coexist. "You cannot prompt motion or emotion into a still, you prompt the physical consequence each leaves behind." · `animatic-keyframes.md` s4 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-042** · Emotion without faces: assign one of a face's four jobs per panel; "anatomy only on a decision" (a hand at rest is filler, name the verb: grip, latch, shift, preload, release) and "objects only when they change state" (board the after-state). A substitution table maps fear, resolve, strain, mistake, triumph, anticipation to an object, crop and render note. · `animatic-keyframes.md` s5 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-043** · The loaded frame: one focal point, one emotion, three depth layers each with a stated job (FG frames or obstructs, MG is the act, BG is stakes), zero dead objects; an evidence test keeps an object only if it indicates state, is the stage of action, or bridges to the next panel; angle encodes the beat when there is no face. · `animatic-keyframes.md` s6 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-044** · Structural defence against skim-reading: SKILL.md is intentionally thin and states "Past attempts to write prompts directly from this skill body produced lazy, mush-prone results; the process lives only in the reference files and you load …". It then forces a dramaturgy check and a three-detail audit before output. · `video/SKILL.md` Mandatory reading order · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-045** · Duration discipline: do not cram a 30 s story into a 5 s prompt; default splits (10 s = 2x5, 30 s = 6x5, 60 s = 12x5) with three named model exceptions; every clip names a final image. · `universal-rules.md` U10-U11 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-046** · A six-point pre-send audit (scene formula complete, three-detail on every shot, three-jobs on every shot, motivated camera, readable geometry, five anchors named). Step 2 is "the most violated". · `dramaturgy.md` s15 · CC BY 4.0
- **EX-047** · Common-failure fixes that are wording-level but reusable: "one continuous take instead of montage" is fixed by stating that hard cuts are required; "lighting drifts between clips" is fixed by naming the dominant source and direction and repeating it verbatim in every clip; "weak drama" is fixed by playing the ordinary object with full seriousness. · `video/references/fixes-and-skeletons.md` s2 · CC BY 4.0

**What VS gets wrong or omits.** Prompt-engineering for Seedance/Kling/Veo with Russian-language trigger phrases; nothing on character acting beyond micro-actions, nothing on timing in frames or on 2D/3D animation, no evals. It dresses numeric claims as law (Murch weights are quoted as the editor's rule, not tested here). The "mandatory reading order, DO NOT WRITE A PROMPT WITHOUT THIS" tone is effective but is exactly the kind of imperative a security reviewer flags (benign here; see s9).

## 3. LM · lottiefiles/motion-design-skill (MIT) · UI/motion focus

**Files read:** `LICENSE`, `skills/motion-design/SKILL.md`, all eight `director/*.md`, `reference/quality-checklist.md`, `reference/troubleshooting.md`.
**Not read:** `patterns/*` (entrance-exit, multi-element, state-feedback, ambient), `reference/timing-easing-tables.md`, `reference/property-selection.md`, `README.md`.

- **EX-048** · Three pillars before any technical decision (emotional intent, visual narrative setup->action->resolution, motion craft) and three motion layers with amplitude: primary 100%, secondary 30-50% offset 50-100 ms with a different easing, ambient 10-20% continuous. "Primary-only animation feels flat." · `skills/motion-design/director/core-philosophy.md` (https://github.com/lottiefiles/motion-design-skill/blob/main/skills/motion-design/director/core-philosophy.md) · MIT
- **EX-049** · An emotion-to-motion table (joy, calm, urgency, sadness, surprise, elegance, playfulness, confidence, curiosity, tenderness) giving character, path, easing and duration range, plus "path as language" (angular = tense, curved = friendly, spiral = whimsical, diagonal = purposeful, vertical up = growth, down = settling). · `director/emotion-mapping.md` · MIT
- **EX-050** · Pick ONE motion personality per project (Playful / Premium / Corporate / Energetic) and keep to it for 90%; define brand motion identity as three constants: one signature easing for 80% of moves, a three-tier duration palette, one entrance pattern. Mixing: ease into a borrowed archetype, never snap. · `director/motion-personality.md` · MIT
- **EX-051** · Disney's 12 principles restated with numbers: anticipation 100-200 ms at 10-20% of the main move (skip under 150 ms), follow-through child delay 50-150 ms, secondary action 30-50% amplitude, squash/stretch skipped for premium, exaggeration scaled per personality, "never linear for spatial movement". · `director/disney-principles.md` · MIT
- **EX-052** · Choreography: lead with the hero; all elements enter from one spatial origin; counter-motion ratios (hero moves left, background shifts right at 20-30%); two "1/3 rules" (no motion travels more than a third of the screen without a keyframe change; with 3+ elements at most a third in motion at once); stagger budgets whose total stays under 500 ms. · `director/choreography.md` · MIT
- **EX-053** · Four-act micro-narrative (anticipation, action, reaction, resolution) with proportions scaled to total duration (under 200 ms drops anticipation and reaction), and "100-200 ms of stillness after resolution before new motion". · `director/narrative-structure.md` · MIT
- **EX-054** · A severity-tiered quality checklist (CRITICAL, HIGH, MEDIUM) and a symptom-to-cause troubleshooting list ("looks robotic" = linear easing or synced starts; "feels cheap" = only primary layer; "too distracting" = too many movers), closed by a ten-point diagnostic whose last item is "fine on the 100th viewing?". · `reference/quality-checklist.md`, `reference/troubleshooting.md` · MIT
- **EX-055** · Weight and material scales duration and overshoot (rigid 1.2x/0%, elastic 0.8x/15-25%, fluid 1.5x/5%, gas 2.0x/0%, heavy = 300-500 ms/0% overshoot). A cheap way to make "this thing is heavy" a number. · `SKILL.md` Easing Selection, Weight Classification · MIT
- **EX-056** · Context adaptation as a table: platform duration modifiers, reduced-motion substitutions (slide becomes fade, parallax static, no auto-play loops), vestibular triggers to avoid, content-type defaults (healthcare = calm and very low density). · `director/context-adaptation.md` · MIT

**What LM gets wrong or omits.** It is UI motion, not film or character animation: no camera, no blocking, no character acting, no scene or beat structure beyond four micro-acts. Millisecond values and percentages carry no source or test evidence. "Never opacity-only" and "always three layers" are stated as CRITICAL with no failure evidence.

## 11. What our skill must do better (imperative rules, each citing the entries it rests on)

1. **Start from a beat sheet whose rows are pressure changes, not shots.** Require state in/out, a named agent for every delta, an arithmetic pressure curve, and the shot-family tag; refuse a row whose visual-action cell contains a camera term. (EX-006, EX-007, EX-029, EX-038)
2. **Block before you frame, with numbers.** Write every move as `start -> motion -> end` with metres/degrees and seconds, name who holds and who moves, and put the geometry in the keyframe before the camera. (EX-001, EX-009, EX-010, EX-015, EX-032)
3. **Give every shot an end state, a movement reason, and one dominant move.** Include an eye-trace target in the first 0.3 s and a hold budget taken out of the shot length first. (EX-002, EX-004, EX-033, EX-037, EX-065)
4. **Make acting observable.** Express emotion as 2-4 body cues, with hold time and eye line, never as an adjective, and add the "head lower / lift with an eyeline change" and weight-shift defaults for single-beat performances. (EX-003, EX-016, EX-030, EX-042)
5. **Fill the gap nobody fills: character acting for rigged, hand-drawn and 3D animation.** None of the eight covers anticipation-hold-overshoot for a *character*, spacing charts, or timing in frames. Adapt the Disney numbers (EX-051), the "shooting on threes then ones" idea (EX-071) and weight/material scales (EX-055) into character-scale rules, and label them as our own, unsourced until tested.
6. **Carry an animatic method with density rules.** Use the keyframe card (implied camera, emotion-via-object, motion-as-still), density ladder (loudness = panel count, one quiet panel between bursts), frame-level hold lengths, and the 15/30 s slices as defaults. (EX-039, EX-040, EX-041, EX-083, EX-034, EX-035)
7. **Choose the arc before the beat map**, and say which arc was chosen and why; do not run two. (EX-038)
8. **Use options-not-questions at intake, through AskUserQuestion**, with a recommended option, tiers, and vague-word translation; ask at most one blocking question and otherwise state assumptions. (EX-022, EX-057, EX-058, EX-060, EX-078)
9. **Define "done" as scored gates with blockers**, per stage: plan, animatic, motion pass, sequence; thresholds committed in advance; a clip cannot pass with a blocker whatever its score; watch with sound off and sound only. (EX-021, EX-054, EX-046)
10. **Ship a failure manual and a cost ladder.** Code the common failures, diagnose the mechanism before rewriting, find the single upstream decision, and after three failures change the shot not the prompt. (EX-017, EX-018, EX-019)
11. **List what the tool cannot do and give a substitution for each**, so the story survives. Keep the list dated and re-testable. (EX-016, EX-020, EX-092)
12. **Make sound part of direction**: frame-pinned sound table, picture lock before sound, re-pin after any length change, one continuous bed to hide drift. (EX-077, EX-035)
13. **Write rules as rule + precedent + self-check**, and require a written note whenever a rule is deliberately broken. Keep numbering append-only. (EX-075, EX-025)
14. **Ship evals**: prompts with assertions checkable from the response text, graded in a separate session from the one that generated the answer; one case per fresh session. (EX-025) None of the others except DS has any.
15. **Hide complexity from the client but keep the spec exact**: describe motion in feel words, keep the technical block alongside, and always end with the next step. (EX-059, EX-061, EX-062)
